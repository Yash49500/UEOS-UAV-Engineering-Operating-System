# Purpose

This document defines the responsibilities, authority, ownership, and decision-making responsibilities of the Research Engineer throughout the engineering lifecycle.

The Research Engineer provides technical evidence that supports engineering decisions by evaluating existing technologies, scientific literature, commercial products, and engineering best practices.

---

# Role Overview

The Research Engineer investigates engineering problems before design begins.

The role identifies existing solutions, benchmarks competing approaches, evaluates technologies, performs trade studies, and recommends engineering options supported by objective evidence.

The Research Engineer does not design the final system but provides the technical foundation upon which design decisions are made.

---

# Primary Responsibilities

## Literature Review

The Research Engineer shall:

- Review scientific literature.
- Review conference papers.
- Review technical reports.
- Review standards.
- Review manufacturer documentation.
- Identify state-of-the-art solutions.

### Deliverables

- Literature Review Report
- Annotated Bibliography

---

## State-of-the-Art (SoA) Analysis

The Research Engineer shall:

- Identify existing UAV solutions.
- Analyse current research trends.
- Identify leading technologies.
- Evaluate maturity of available solutions.

### Deliverables

- State-of-the-Art Analysis

---

## Benchmarking

The Research Engineer shall benchmark:

- Commercial UAV platforms
- Academic projects
- Open-source solutions
- Government research
- Industrial systems

Benchmark criteria may include:

- Performance
- Weight
- Power Consumption
- Cost
- Accuracy
- Reliability
- Complexity

### Deliverables

- Benchmark Report

---

## Trade Studies

The Research Engineer shall perform structured comparisons between alternative solutions.

Trade studies may include:

- Sensors
- Flight Controllers
- Cameras
- AI Models
- Tracking Algorithms
- Communication Systems
- Embedded Computers
- Batteries
- Airframes

Each trade study shall evaluate:

- Advantages
- Disadvantages
- Cost
- Performance
- Risks
- Integration Effort

### Deliverables

- Trade Study Report

---

## Technology Evaluation

Evaluate candidate technologies using objective engineering criteria.

Examples include:

- Computer Vision Frameworks
- Navigation Algorithms
- SLAM Systems
- Tracking Algorithms
- Neural Networks
- Flight Controllers
- Communication Protocols

### Deliverables

- Technology Evaluation Report

---

## Component Evaluation

Assess hardware components for project suitability.

Examples:

- Cameras
- IMUs
- GPS Modules
- LiDAR
- Companion Computers
- ESCs
- Motors
- Batteries

Evaluation criteria include:

- Performance
- Size
- Weight
- Power
- Availability
- Cost
- Reliability

### Deliverables

- Component Evaluation Report

---

## Algorithm Evaluation

Evaluate algorithms against project requirements.

Examples:

- Object Detection
- Object Tracking
- Path Planning
- Sensor Fusion
- Obstacle Avoidance
- Control Algorithms

Evaluation criteria include:

- Accuracy
- Speed
- Computational Cost
- Memory Usage
- Robustness
- Scalability

### Deliverables

- Algorithm Comparison Report

---

## Technology Readiness Assessment

Assess maturity using Technology Readiness Levels (TRLs).

Typical considerations:

- Research Prototype
- Laboratory Validation
- Flight Validation
- Operational Deployment

### Deliverables

- TRL Assessment Report

---

## Research Gap Analysis

Identify limitations in current technologies and opportunities for innovation.

### Deliverables

- Research Gap Analysis

---

## Technical Recommendations

Provide evidence-based recommendations to support engineering decisions.

Recommendations shall include:

- Supporting evidence
- Trade-offs
- Risks
- Assumptions
- Expected impact

### Deliverables

- Technical Recommendation Report

---

# Decision Authority

The Research Engineer has authority to:

- Recommend technologies.
- Reject unsupported technical claims.
- Recommend additional research.
- Identify insufficient evidence.
- Request clarification of research objectives.

Final engineering decisions remain the responsibility of the Chief Systems Engineer.

# Responsibility Boundaries

The Research Engineer is responsible for:

- Research quality.
- Evidence collection.
- Technology evaluation.
- Benchmarking.
- Trade studies.
- Technical recommendations.

The Research Engineer is **not** responsible for implementation or final engineering approval.

---

# Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Performance is measured using:

- Research Coverage
- Source Credibility
- Benchmark Completeness
- Trade Study Quality
- Recommendation Acceptance Rate
- Evidence Traceability
- Timeliness of Research Deliverables

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Recommending technologies without evidence.
- Using outdated or unverified sources.
- Ignoring competing approaches.
- Focusing only on academic research.
- Ignoring practical implementation constraints.
- Presenting opinions as engineering facts.

---

# Best Practices

- Use multiple independent sources.
- Compare alternatives objectively.
- Document assumptions explicitly.
- Quantify comparisons whenever possible.
- Highlight limitations of available evidence.
- Keep research reproducible and well referenced.
