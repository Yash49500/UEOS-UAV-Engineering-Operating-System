# Purpose

This document defines the responsibilities, authority, ownership, and decision-making responsibilities of the Chief Systems Engineer (CSE) throughout the complete UAV engineering lifecycle.

The CSE is responsible for ensuring that the final system satisfies the mission while maintaining technical integrity across all engineering disciplines.

---

# Role Overview

The Chief Systems Engineer is the technical leader of the project.

Unlike discipline-specific engineers, the CSE focuses on the complete system rather than individual components.

The CSE coordinates engineering activities, resolves technical conflicts, manages system-level decisions, and ensures that engineering work aligns with mission objectives.

---

# Primary Responsibilities

## Mission Definition

The CSE shall:

- Define the engineering mission.
- Understand stakeholder needs.
- Identify operational scenarios.
- Define mission success criteria.
- Establish project objectives.

### Deliverables

- Mission Definition
- Stakeholder Analysis
- Operational Constraints

---

## Requirements Engineering

The CSE shall:

- Approve system requirements.
- Ensure requirements are measurable.
- Ensure requirements are traceable.
- Resolve conflicting requirements.
- Approve the System Requirements Specification (SRS).

### Deliverables

- SRS
- Requirement Traceability Matrix

---

## System Architecture

The CSE shall:

- Approve the overall system architecture.
- Allocate functions to subsystems.
- Approve subsystem interfaces.
- Review architectural trade-offs.
- Ensure architecture satisfies requirements.

### Deliverables

- System Architecture
- Functional Decomposition
- Interface Definitions

---

## Technical Leadership

The CSE shall:

- Coordinate engineering teams.
- Conduct technical reviews.
- Resolve technical disagreements.
- Maintain engineering standards.
- Monitor project technical progress.

---

## Risk Management

The CSE shall:

- Identify engineering risks.
- Maintain the project risk register.
- Define mitigation strategies.
- Review high-risk design decisions.
- Escalate unacceptable risks.

### Deliverables

- Risk Register
- Risk Mitigation Plan

---

## Verification & Validation

The CSE shall:

- Approve verification plans.
- Review validation results.
- Ensure every requirement is verified.
- Approve system readiness for deployment.

### Deliverables

- Verification Matrix
- Validation Report
- System Acceptance Report

---

# Decision Authority

The Chief Systems Engineer has authority to approve:

- Mission Definition
- System Requirements
- System Architecture
- Interface Definitions
- Engineering Standards
- Technical Trade Studies
- Design Reviews
- Verification Strategy
- Deployment Readiness

# Responsibility Boundaries

The Chief Systems Engineer is responsible for:

- System-level engineering decisions.
- Engineering coordination.
- Requirement allocation.
- Architecture approval.
- Technical risk management.
- Engineering quality.

The Chief Systems Engineer is **not** responsible for implementing individual subsystems unless acting in an additional engineering role.

- Validation Success Rate

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Designing before defining requirements.
- Selecting components before architecture.
- Ignoring requirement traceability.
- Skipping design reviews.
- Allowing undocumented engineering decisions.
- Delaying risk identification.
- Poor communication between engineering teams.

---

# Best Practices

- Think in systems, not components.
- Base every decision on requirements.
- Document every major engineering decision.
- Review architecture before implementation.
- Maintain traceability throughout the lifecycle.
- Conduct regular design reviews.
- Prioritise safety and reliability.
